# Trainer Notes
## THHHOK0971B: Provide Housekeeping Services to Guests

---

## Trainer Preparation
Before the lesson, the trainer should:

- Review the competency standard and ensure alignment with hotel procedures
- Prepare examples of guest requests, complaint forms, and room change checklists
- Gather sample equipment used in accommodation rooms
- Set up role-play scenarios for complaints and room changes
- Confirm reporting lines and escalation procedures in the property

---

## Delivery Tips

### 1. Set a Professional Service Tone
Trainees should understand that housekeeping staff are often the first point of contact for guests in a problem or service request. Tone, timing, and professionalism matter just as much as technical skill.

### 2. Emphasize Service Recovery
A guest may be dissatisfied because of a service failure, delay, or misunderstanding. The trainer should stress that the best response is:
- acknowledge promptly
- apologize appropriately
- offer a solution
- record the matter
- escalate when needed

### 3. Make Note-Taking a Habit
Trainees should learn to take accurate notes using clear, factual language. Good records protect both the guest and the property.

### 4. Explain Authority Boundaries
The trainer should clarify the difference between issues staff can resolve and issues that must be escalated. Example:
- Staff can replace towels or deliver a requested item
- Staff should escalate maintenance issues, security concerns, or major guest dissatisfaction

### 5. Focus on Courtesy and Guest Confidence
Even in minor issues, guests expect to feel heard. Calm, respectful language builds confidence and prevents escalation.

---

## Suggested Teaching Methods

### A. Demonstration
Show how to:
- record a request on a log sheet
- explain room equipment use to a guest
- handle a complaint without blame or defensiveness
- prepare a room move checklist

### B. Role-Play
Role-play scenarios to help trainees practice:
- a guest asking for an extra blanket
- a guest reporting a malfunctioning kettle
- a complaint about dirty room conditions
- a room change request because the guest is disturbed by noise

### C. Group Discussion
Discuss what the staff member should do, what the supervisor should be informed of, and what the guest should be told.

### D. Practical Scenario Planning
Use a real guest scenario and walk trainees through the correct sequence of action.

---

## Detailed Facilitation Guide

### Module 1: Handling Housekeeping Requests
Trainer focus:
- Requests must be handled calmly, especially when the guest is impatient or frustrated
- Confirm key information before acting
- Ensure follow-up is communicated clearly

Key questions to ask:
- What details must be recorded?
- When should a staff member apologize?
- Who should be informed if the request cannot be completed on time?

### Module 2: Advising Guests on Equipment
Trainer focus:
- Equipment guidance should be courteous and practical
- Malfunctions must be reported immediately
- If replacement is not possible, offer an alternative solution

Key questions to ask:
- What should you do if a guest cannot operate the kettle?
- What if the safe is not working?
- When should equipment be collected?

### Module 3: Guest Complaints
Trainer focus:
- Speed matters
- The complaint should be treated seriously even if it seems minor
- The employee should stay calm, listen, and act without being defensive

Key questions to ask:
- What are the steps to complaint handling?
- What should be recorded?
- When should the matter be escalated?

### Module 4: Guest Incidents
Trainer focus:
- Incidents require immediate attention and accurate reporting
- Staff should follow property procedures, not improvise
- Guest communication should remain polite and reassuring

### Module 5: Room Change Handling
Trainer focus:
- The room change must be accurate and low-stress for the guest
- All belongings must be transferred safely and without damage
- The guest should be updated, reassured, and thanked

Key questions to ask:
- What should you do before moving the guest?
- What if the guest is not happy with the new room?
- How do you verify guest property is protected?

---

## Common Mistakes to Correct
- Not confirming the request details
- Making promises that cannot be fulfilled
- Failing to document a complaint or incident
- Speaking in a rushed or impatient way
- Not reporting a malfunction or safety issue
- Moving guest property without checking or protecting it
- Ignoring guest comfort during room changes

---

## Suggested Discussion Prompts
- Why is documentation important in guest service?
- What could make a guest feel dismissed during a complaint?
- How do you maintain professionalism under pressure?
- Why is room change communication important even when the move is simple?

---

## Assessment Guidance
Observe whether trainees:
- speak clearly and respectfully
- note key details accurately
- respond quickly and appropriately
- understand escalation points
- protect guest property and hotel standards

---

## Final Trainer Message
Housekeeping staff are central to the guest experience. The goal is not only to complete tasks, but to do so in a way that makes the guest feel safe, respected, informed, and cared for.
